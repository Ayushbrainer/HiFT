# HiFT on NAT2021: experiment log and logbook

How this file is kept
- It is the project logbook: every step taken, decision made, hypothesis, question and mistake gets an entry in the **Logbook** section at the bottom (chronological, newest last), with the reason and the outcome. Experiments also have their own sections (E0, E1, ...) with the full tables.
- Planned ideas live under **Next steps** as P1, P2, ... and move to an E-section when they are run.
- Entries are dated; a clock time is added only where it is known. Job IDs link an entry to the files `output/output_<name>_<id>.out` and `error/error_<name>_<id>.err`.
- Claim only what was measured. Hypotheses are marked as hypotheses.

Tracker: HiFT, `tools/snapshot/first.pth` (the README's general_model), result tracker name `first1.2` (w1 = 1.2).
Metric: one-pass evaluation (OPE) success AUC and precision at 20 px, computed by `tools/eval_nat.py`.
All runs go through SLURM (`job_*.sh`), logs in `output/` and `error/`.

Result folders: `tools/results/<dataset>/<tracker name>/<sequence>.txt`.

- Enhancing both crops gave about +0.06 success over baseline (roughly +22% relative).

- CLAHE costs about 20% of throughput, gamma almost nothing.

Caveats: 30 sequences, single run each, test-set sequences (screening only, not a final claim), no significance test.

## E2: the three "both" settings on all 180 sequences (done)

Job 48452 (`job_enh_full.sh`), result folders `first1.2_gamma_both_180`, `first1.2_clahe_both_180`, `first1.2_gc_both_180`.
Settings identical to E1 (gamma 0.4, clip 3.0), run on all 180 NAT2021 sequences.

Why: the E1 differences between gamma, CLAHE and gamma+CLAHE are within what 30 sequences can resolve. Expectation going in: gamma+CLAHE should be at least as good as each alone because they do different jobs (global exposure vs local contrast). The full run tests that.

How the result will be judged: `tools/eval_nat.py --compare` gives the mean per-sequence AUC difference for every pair with a 95% paired bootstrap interval (resampling sequences) and the number of sequences won. A pair counts as different only if the interval excludes 0. Per-sequence AUCs are saved to `tools/illumination/E2_per_sequence_auc.csv`.

Note on noise: re-running the same configuration on the same data should give nearly the same numbers (the tracker is deterministic apart from small GPU effects). The variation in E1 comes from the small sample of sequences, not from run-to-run randomness, so the full 180 set and the paired bootstrap are the right tools. I have not verified run-to-run repeatability; it could be checked by repeating one run.

## E3c: fragility test on both halves (done)

Job 48501 (`job_fragility.sh`). Hypothesis to test: HiFT's per-sequence success is fragile, meaning small input changes can swing a sequence's AUC a lot, in either direction, so the "improved" and "worse" halves from E2b are partly luck of the gamma 0.4 run (regression to the mean) and not a property of the sequences.

Sample: 20 random sequences from the half that improved with gamma 0.4 and 20 from the half that got worse (tolerance +/-0.01, fixed seed). Lists: `tools/illumination/E3c_improved_list.txt`, `E3c_worse_list.txt`.

Runs on the same 40 sequences, enhancement on both template and search crop:
- repeat of the unmodified baseline (`_f_repeat_baseline`): if it differs from the saved baseline, the tracker is not deterministic;
- gamma only at 0.95, 0.9, 0.8, 0.6, 0.5 (0.95 / 0.9 are tiny perturbations);
- CLAHE only at clip 1, 2, 4;
- the existing full-set runs gamma 0.4 and CLAHE clip 3 reused for these 40 sequences.

What would support fragility
- the repeat run changes nothing, but gamma 0.95 / CLAHE clip 1 (almost no visible change) already move many sequences by large amounts;
- the two halves' mean change goes toward 0 or flips sign under settings other than the one that defined them (regression to the mean);
- the change per sequence correlates weakly across settings (the direction is not a property of the sequence).

What would argue against it: the repeat and tiny perturbations leave results almost unchanged, and the halves keep their signs across settings, which would mean the effect really belongs to those sequences.

Outputs: `tools/illumination/E3c_per_sequence_auc.csv`, `tools/illumination/fragility.png`, summary table in the job output.

Interim result (from the saved box files, before AUC is computed; repeat, gamma 0.95, 0.9, 0.8 finished, 40 sequences each):
- The repeat of the baseline is byte-identical to the saved baseline in 40/40 sequences: the tracker is deterministic.
- Every perturbed run differs from the baseline from frame 2 onward. Comparing the top-left box corner (sum of |dx| + |dy|, pixels): gamma 0.95 makes 33/40 sequences differ by more than 10 px at some point, 31/40 by more than 50 px, and 17/40 end more than 50 px apart. Gamma 0.9: 34 / 31 / 13. Gamma 0.8: 35 / 32 / 21.
- Among the sequences that split by more than 50 px, the first split happens a median of about a third of the way through the sequence.
- The amount of divergence does not grow with the strength of the change (0.95 is almost no change: dark values move up by a couple of levels).
- Caveats: this measures box position, not AUC; box corner differences can be large for large targets even when both boxes are on the target; 40 sequences, 4 runs so far.

Figure: `tools/illumination/fragility.png`; per-sequence AUCs: `tools/illumination/E3c_per_sequence_auc.csv`.

Findings
- Deterministic: the repeat of the baseline is identical (all differences exactly 0).
- Box positions diverge easily (interim result above: gamma 0.95 already splits 31/40 tracks by more than 50 px), but AUC moves much less for tiny changes: at gamma 0.95 mean |dAUC| is 0.024 and only 10% of sequences change by more than 0.05.
- The "halves were mostly luck" hypothesis is not supported. The improved half improves under every setting (+0.03 up to +0.17, growing with strength), and the per-sequence change correlates strongly with the gamma 0.4 change for gamma 0.8 / 0.6 / 0.5 (0.73 / 0.90 / 0.94). So much of the effect belongs to the sequences.
- The worse half is not hurt by mild gamma (0.95 / 0.9 / 0.8: about 0.00) and is hurt by strong settings (gamma 0.6 / 0.5 / 0.4: -0.03 to -0.05; CLAHE clip 3-4: -0.07 to -0.10). That points to over-enhancement for those sequences.
- Gamma 0.8 keeps about 85% of the improved-half gain of gamma 0.4 (+0.143 vs +0.168) with no loss in the worse half.
- Best on this sample: gamma 0.8 and 0.6 (success 0.452). CLAHE clip 4 is worst (0.387).
- Both of the two effects exist: sensitivity to tiny changes (shown by box positions and by sequences with large single-sequence swings in the figure) and a systematic dose-dependent effect of enhancement.

Caveats
- The sample is balanced by construction (20 improved-half, 20 worse-half), so absolute numbers do not represent the full set; use it for comparing settings with each other.
- The halves were defined using the gamma 0.4 run, which biases comparisons involving that run and settings correlated with it.
- 40 sequences, one run per setting, no bootstrap yet. A gamma 0.8 / 0.6 full-set run (180 sequences) is needed before claiming an improvement over gamma 0.4.

## E6: break the feedback loop (done)

Job 48605 (`job_reinit.sh`). Hypothesis (mechanism 1 in the fragility discussion, not yet tested): HiFT's result is sensitive to tiny input changes mainly because each frame's predicted box decides the next crop, so a small early difference compounds and there is no recovery. Test: every N frames, put the tracker's box and scale back on the ground truth after that frame's prediction was recorded. The original template is kept (mode `box`), so only the feedback loop is cut and the template is not refreshed. Diagnostic only: it uses the ground truth, so AUC values go up and the tracker is not usable this way.

Implementation: `tools/test.py --reinit_every N [--reinit_mode box|full]`, `HiFTTracker.reset_box()`. `full` (which also rebuilds the template) exists but is not used in this job.

Design: balanced 40 sequence sample from E3c; three runs per condition (baseline, gamma 0.95 = almost no change, gamma 0.6 = best setting) for reset every 200 frames and every 50 frames; the no-reset runs already exist (`first1.2`, `first1.2_f_gamma0.95`, `first1.2_f_gamma0.6`). ## E8: anchored and gated template update (P15 follow-up, done)

Job 48615 (`job_p15.sh`). E7 showed that rebuilding the template from the last 3 frames hurts (0.334 vs 0.452 at gamma 0.6); the leading untested explanation is drift (the predicted box feeds the template and frame 1 is gone after 3 updates). Two ways to limit drift:
- **Anchor:** keep the frame 1 template and blend, `template = (1 - a) * frame 1 features + a * mean(last 3 features)`, with a = 0.5 and 0.2.
- **Gate:** update only when this frame's best score is at least the median of the sequence's earlier scores (every frame updates during the first 10 frames). The scale of the tracker's confidence score is not known, so the gate is relative to the sequence itself; a fixed threshold was not possible without seeing the score distribution.

Implementation: `HiFTTemplateTracker(alpha=..., gate=...)` in `pysot/tracker/hift_tracker_tmpl.py` (with `alpha=None, gate='none'` it is exactly the E7 tracker), flags `--tmpl_alpha` and `--tmpl_gate` in `tools/test_tmpl.py`. Feature averaging only (the pixel average was no better in E7).

Runs (gamma 0.6 on both crops, 40 sequence sample from E3c, k = 3): `_p15_a0.5`, `_p15_a0.2`, `_p15_a0.5_gate`, `_p15_a0.2_gate`, `_p15_gate_only` (gate without anchor, to separate the two effects). References on the same sequences: frame 1 template `first1.2_f_gamma0.6` (0.452) and the E7 feature average without anchor or gate `first1.2_t_feat_g0.6` (0.334). Output: `tools/illumination/E8_per_sequence_auc.csv` and a paired bootstrap table.

   (a) average the last 3 backbone features (`model.zf`, which `ModelBuilder.template` stores) rather than the pixels;
   (b) blend with the original frame-1 template, `T = (1 - a) * T0 + a * mean(last 3)`, so the trusted anchor is never lost;
   (c) update only on confident frames; needs the distribution of `best_score` first (it is not saved in the result files now).
   Pixel averaging of the crops would also blur them when the box is off, so feature averaging is preferred. The test is both accuracy (AUC) and sensitivity (mean |dAUC| between baseline and gamma 0.95). Do after E6.

P16. **Retrain / fine-tune with the enhancement inside the training pipeline (user's plan, 2026-10-29).** Reasoning recorded: if the weights are retrained on dark frames and gamma / CLAHE are then applied only at test time, the test inputs move away from the training inputs (mismatch the other way round; hypothesis, not measured), so the gain seen with the daytime-trained model would probably vanish or turn negative. Consistent version: apply the same gamma and CLAHE in training and at test.
   - Hook: `Augmentation.__call__` in `pysot/datasets/augmentation.py` returns the cropped, resized patch (127 template / 287 search) after shift, scale, color, blur and flip; apply night simulation and the enhancement there, on the same crop sizes as `get_subwindow` at test time (`pysot/utils/enhance.py`).
   - If dark data is synthesised from daytime images, the darkening must include realistic noise (for example Poisson plus Gaussian noise that does not shrink with brightness) and compression; otherwise the enhanced training crops are clean while real enhanced night crops are noisy.
   - Consider randomizing gamma (for example 0.4-0.8) and clip (1-4) in training so the model is not tied to one value (also relevant to the fragility seen in E3c).
   - Pick the values for evaluation on a validation split, not on the test set; the best gamma for a retrained model may differ from 0.6.
   - Training needs boxes; NAT2021-train is unlabeled (pseudo-labels, synthetic data, or a UDAT-style adaptation). Cluster limits: one GPU, 6 h per job, so chained checkpointed jobs.
   - Check the output size the pretrained weights were trained with: `experiments/config.yaml` sets TRAIN OUTPUT_SIZE 11 while the default in `pysot/core/config.py` is 21.
   - Cheap test once new weights exist: same 40 sequence sample, no enhancement vs gamma 0.8 vs gamma 0.6 with the new `--snapshot`.

Evaluation rule for all of these: balanced 40 sequence sample first for screening, then all 180 with the paired bootstrap; tune on one half of the sequences and confirm on the other.
